# How ParentVeda talks in Pregnancy — the voice guide

Written 2026-09-29 for the pregnancy warmth pass. Source of truth for WHAT to change is the pregnancy gap
analysis (`Pregnancy-Gap-Analysis-Flo-WhatToExpect-vs-ParentVeda.pdf`, version 1, 29 September 2026, and its
`.xlsx`). This file is HOW every word in the pregnancy stage is written while that work is done: reads, door
tiles and heroes, daily cards, tools, buttons, empty screens, closing notes.

It is the trying-to-conceive voice (`docs/TTC-VOICE.md`, approved by the user 2026-09-26) carried across, with the
reader changed and the pregnancy rules from the PDF added. Where the two guides agree, this one does not repeat
the reasoning.

The user's brief, in their words: *warm, comforting, hugging, safe, reassuring; she understands it in one go and
is never confused; it must not feel written by AI.*

---

## 1. Who we are talking to

A woman in India who is expecting a baby. Some weeks she is excited, some weeks she is tired, sick or scared, and
often she is reading at night with a question she felt silly asking anyone. Her mother, her mother-in-law and the
internet are all telling her different things. Sometimes her husband is reading over her shoulder.

We sound like **an older sister who happens to be a good doctor's friend.** Warm, calm, on her side. We tell her
the truth gently and we tell her what to do next. We are never a textbook, a lecture or a sales page.

## 2. The rules

1. **Talk to her as "you".** "Your baby", "your bump", "your doctor". Never "a woman", "the mother" or "she" when
   we mean the reader. "You both" when it is about the couple.
2. **Everyday contractions:** it's, you're, don't, isn't, you'll, baby's.
3. **Short sentences, one idea each.** About 15 words on average. Paragraphs of two or three sentences.
4. **Plain words first, the medical word second, in brackets:** "the dark line (linea nigra)". The medical word
   leads only when it is the word printed on her report.
5. **Say the comforting true thing early.** If most women feel this, or it usually settles, say so in the first
   lines. She came with a worry; answer the worry before the biology.
6. **Name the feeling once, then help.** One short line ("This can feel like a lot"), then something useful. Never
   a paragraph of sympathy, never "we understand how you feel".
7. **Reassure with facts, not cheerleading.** "Most marks fade to a soft silver within a year" comforts her.
   "You'll be glowing, mama!" does not.
8. **Never make her feel at fault.** Nothing that hints she caused a mark, a symptom or a complication. When her
   family's advice is wrong we correct it kindly, because the person who told her loves her.
9. **No dashes as punctuation.** No em dashes (—) and no spaced hyphens ( - ). Use a full stop, comma, colon or
   brackets. Ranges use "to": "6 to 12 months". Hyphenated words (pregnancy-safe) are fine.
10. **No CAPITALS for emphasis**, no exclamation marks, no emoji.
11. **No clever lines.** No punchlines, no "it's not X, it's Y" sayings, no wise-sounding closers.
12. **Doctor lines stay calm and exact.** Say when to call and how soon, without alarm. Every "today", "straight
    away" or "go to hospital" keeps exactly its urgency. Never soften a warning sign into a maybe.
13. **Screen text is short.** Buttons are verbs ("Check an ingredient", "See the cream"). Empty screens invite her
    in and say what she'll get. Tiles say what is inside in the words she would search.
14. **Indian where it helps:** chemist, dupatta, mother-in-law, Indian summers, rupees, dal and roti. No American-only
    references.
15. **About the same length.** A rewrite stays within about 15% of the original unless it is adding content the
    gap analysis asks for. Warmer does not mean longer.

## 3. What the PDF adds for pregnancy

- **A short answer at the top of every read** ("Behind · How reads are written"): two or three sentences that
  answer the title, in the reader's `shortAnswer` box. She gets the answer in ten seconds and reads on if she wants.
- **Headings as her questions** where a read explains something ("Why does this happen?", "What helps?", "When
  should I call my doctor?"). Short labels stay short where a heading is a list name ("Morning", "Night").
- **Never the baby's sex.** No piece, tip or line suggests learning the baby's sex before birth (PCPNDT Act).
- **No claim we can't back.** No invented reviewer, no "research-backed" or "clinically proven" unless a real,
  named review happened. Bylines stay "ParentVeda editorial" until a real doctor has read the piece.
- **One medical line where she starts:** "ParentVeda explains and reminds. Your doctor decides."

## 4. The banned list

Same as `docs/TTC-VOICE.md` §4, plus the pregnancy-specific ones.

**Words and phrases:** journey (as a metaphor), navigate, empower, delve, embark, holistic, game-changer, crucial,
you've got this, rest assured, we understand how you feel, remember (as an opener), it's important to note, it's
worth noting, let's dive in, genuinely, actually, quietly, simply, truly (as fillers), "the single most", "the one
thing nobody tells you", "here's the thing", mama, mommy, momma, miracle, magical, blessed (as our word; hers is fine
if she says it), "don't worry".

**Habits:** exclamation marks and emoji; opening with a pile of questions; lists of three everywhere; dashes as
punctuation; capitals for emphasis; punchlines; jokes at hard moments (loss, a scary result, a complication);
mocking family advice; sounding like an advert on a page about safety.

## 5. What must never change

- **Facts.** Numbers, doses, timings, weeks, percentages, sources and warning signs stay exactly as they are.
- **Safety.** Every "see a doctor" line keeps its full meaning and urgency. We never diagnose, never give a
  personal probability and never contradict her own doctor.
- **Anything the code relies on.** Ids, keys, enum names, route names, saved values stay untouched.
- **The Hindi side** of any existing pair. New work is English (CLAUDE.md, 2026-08-27).
- **Flo's and What to Expect's words.** They show us what to cover. We never copy a sentence; no run of eight
  words may match theirs.

## 6. Before and after, from our own pregnancy copy

**1. A door hero** (Belly & skin)
> Before: What is happening, what actually helps, and what is safe to put on your skin — cared for simply.
>
> After: What's happening to your skin, what helps, and what's safe to put on it.

**2. Opening a read** (Why stretch marks happen)
> Before: How much any one body marks depends mostly on skin type, genetics, and how much and how fast the bump
> grows, not on effort.
>
> After: How much you mark depends mostly on your skin type, your genes and how fast your bump grows. It isn't
> about effort.

**3. A tip line** (a safe routine)
> Before: That is genuinely enough. Pregnancy is not the time to add a ten-step routine; it is the time to simplify
> to what is known to be safe.
>
> After: That's enough. Pregnancy isn't the time for a ten-step routine. Keep it to a few things you know are safe.

**4. A warning** (itching)
> Before: Do not wait this out. If the itching is intense, worse at night, or on your palms and soles, call your
> doctor today rather than at your next scheduled visit.
>
> After: Please don't wait this out. If the itching is intense, worse at night, or on your palms and soles, call
> your doctor today. Don't wait for your next visit.

## 7. How each rewrite is checked

1. Read against this guide: rules, banned list, no dashes (a script greps the changed strings).
2. Facts compared line by line with the original: every number, dose, timing and warning sign still there.
3. Ids and keys unchanged. `flutter analyze` clean of new issues; the full suite passes.
4. Nothing copied from Flo or What to Expect: eight-word overlap check against the titles and text in the gap
   analysis.

## 8. Technical notes for the rewrite

- Change only the English. In `_t(en, hi)` pairs change the first string only. Where a file uses
  `LocalizedText(en: s, hi: s)` for English-only copy, both sides carry the same English, so both change together.
- Contractions add apostrophes: use double-quoted Dart strings or escape (`it\'s`).
- Leave code comments alone.
- If unsure whether a string is shown to her or compared by the code, grep its uses first; if still unsure, leave
  it and list it.
- Tests that pin exact text are updated only where the text change was meant. Safety tests are never weakened.
