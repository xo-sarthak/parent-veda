# Market landscape — the four stages, who makes money, and where the gaps are

Researched August 2026. Figures are the latest available; where a number is a
third-party estimate rather than a filing, it says so. Conversions at ₹88/USD.

**Read the credibility note first (§0). A lot of published data in this
category is fabricated, and acting on it would be expensive.**

---

## §0. Credibility note — most "market size" reports here are junk

Published research says the *fertility tracking apps market* was **$99.24bn in
2025, heading to $236.5bn by 2032**.

Flo is the global leader in that category. Flo's 2025 revenue was **$275M**.
Natural Cycles, the #2 by revenue, did **$63M**. Add every named competitor and
you do not clear **$600M**. The report overstates its own market by roughly
**160×**.

The same houses put the *parenting apps market* at $1.69–1.94bn, which is
directionally plausible, and then a sibling report says $1.14bn for 2026. They
do not agree with each other.

**Rule for this project: only company-level numbers count.** Filed accounts
(FirstCry, Skillmatics, Physics Wallah, Ziff Davis), disclosed raises, or
credible trackers (Sacra, Tracxn, Entrackr, Business of Apps). Treat every
"$X billion market growing at Y% CAGR" line as marketing collateral for the
report itself.

| **Maven Clinic** | ~$268M ARR (Sacra), +26% YoY; $1.7B valuation; 2,000+ employer clients, 175 countries (Amazon, Microsoft, AT&T) | **B2B — employer pays** | Largest revenue in the stage, and no consumer pays a rupee |

### India

- **IVF services market: $1.4–2.5bn (₹12,000–22,000 Cr), growing ~16% CAGR.**
  Nova IVF opened its 100th centre in 2025; Indira IVF is deep in tier-2/3.
  A cycle is **₹1.5–3 lakh, cash-pay, uninsured.**
- **Inito** is Indian-built and takes Indian salaries against US revenue. Note
  what that says: the founders looked at the Indian consumer market for their
  own product and went abroad.
- **Gynoveda** — ₹68.14 Cr FY25, **+1% growth**. Ayurvedic PCOS/fertility D2C.
  The flat line is the story: they found the ceiling.
- Indian femtech overall: **476 companies, 139 funded, $271M raised in total,
  ever.** That is less than Flo raised alone.

### Insights

1. **Nobody makes real money selling a TTC app to a consumer.** The revenue in
   this stage sits in three places, none of which is software: **diagnostics
   and consumables** (Inito, Mira, Premom), **employer benefits** (Maven,
   Progyny, Carrot), and **clinics** (Kindbody, Indira, Nova).
2. **The employer-benefits market does not exist in India.** Maven's $268M has
   no Indian analogue and won't for a decade. Indian employers buy group
   mediclaim; fertility is explicitly excluded from nearly all of it.
3. **TTC has the worst cohort economics of the four stages.** Every user exits
   within 3–12 months — pregnant, or into a clinic, or giving up. There is no
   long-tail retention to amortise CAC against. Flo survives this only because
   the same user was already there for periods and stays after.
4. **Flo's paid conversion is ~7% (5M of 70M)** — that is *exceptional* for
   freemium, achieved on global (mostly US/EU) payment behaviour.
5. **In India the stage is drenched in stigma and the decision is not the
   woman's alone.** Product implication: search and content behaviour is
   private and anonymous; anything social fails.

### Takeaway for ParentVeda

TTC is **not a revenue stage. It is an acquisition stage** — and it is the
cheapest place in the whole funnel to acquire a woman who will be worth money
for the next five years.

The one real money line here is **qualified referral into IVF and
diagnostics**. A clinic with a ₹1.5–3 lakh ticket can rationally pay
₹5,000–20,000 for a qualified consultation lead. That is 30–100× the lifetime
value of the same woman as an app subscriber. The honest version of this is a
directory + preparation product that routes her *ready* and *informed* — which
is also the only version compatible with the clinical-ownership rule already in
`CLAUDE.md`.

## §2. Pregnancy (conception → birth)

### Global leaders

| Company | Scale | Model |
|---|---|---|
| **BabyCenter** (Ziff Davis) | 100M+ monthly across app + web, multi-language | **Advertising.** Sits inside Ziff Davis Health & Wellness: **$402.4M FY25 revenue, +11%, $150.0M adj. EBITDA (37% margin)** |
| **What to Expect** (Everyday Health / Ziff Davis) | Same house | Advertising + brand |
| **Peanut** | 12.1M MAU FY25, 8.4M downloads, 68% US/UK/CA | **~$42M ad + partnership revenue FY25 on only $21.8M ever raised.** The most capital-efficient business in the category |
| **Maven Clinic** | $268M ARR | Employer-paid |
| **Ovia** | Labcorp-owned | Employer + health plan |
| **Babylist** | Registry | **Commerce with no inventory risk.** Acquired Expectful (meditation) in 2023 — content bought as a funnel into a registry |
| **Pregnancy+** (Philips) | Very high downloads | Weak monetisation; effectively brand marketing for Philips Avent |

### India

| Company | Outcome |
|---|---|
| **FirstCry / Brainbees** | **₹7,660 Cr revenue FY25 (~$870M), +18%. First-ever net profit. GMV ₹10,585 Cr.** Listed Aug 2024, +40% on debut. **The category's only genuine winner.** Commerce, with content bolted on |
| **Cloudnine** | **~₹2,000 Cr revenue FY25-26 (~$227M), ~₹300 Cr EBITDA. Valued ~₹11,000 Cr (~$1.25bn)** in a live PE stake sale (TPG, Permira circling). Services — the birth itself |
| **Mylo** | $28.4M raised over 9 rounds. Started as a pregnancy/parenting community app; **pivoted to its own D2C product line** — 100+ SKUs, 7 lakh units. 167 employees |
| **BabyChakra** | Sold to Good Glamm. **Good Glamm ceased operations July 2025**; lenders enforced charge; brands being sold off one by one; websites went dark. Gone |
| **Momspresso** | Same house. Gone |
| **Healofy** | Vernacular community + commerce. Alive, small |
| **Parentune** | ~$8.6M revenue, 1M+ downloads, Hindi version launched. Alive, small |

### Insights

1. **Every pure Indian pregnancy content/community app has either died or
   become a product company.** Momspresso and BabyChakra are dead. Mylo sells
   shampoo. That is not four coincidences; it is the arithmetic.
2. **The arithmetic:** Indian content CPMs run roughly ₹80–250 per 1,000
   impressions. A pregnancy user has a hard ~9-month window. Even at heavy
   usage that is **₹15–50 (~$0.20–0.60) of lifetime ad revenue per mother.**
   BabyCenter's model works only because it monetises US and EU impressions.
   **Do not copy an ad-funded model from a market with 20× your CPM.**
3. **Peanut is the exception worth studying** — $42M off $21.8M raised, because
   *brand partnerships* (a Pampers or a Philips paying for a placed programme)
   price off the audience's *value to the advertiser*, not off CPM. That
   travels to India far better than banner inventory does. It is also
   structurally what Brand Studio in this repo already is.
4. **The real money in Indian pregnancy is the birth.** Cloudnine does ₹2,000
   Cr because a delivery is ₹1–3 lakh. The app-adjacent value of a pregnant
   woman in India is not her subscription — it is *which hospital she chooses*
   and *what she buys in her third trimester*.
5. **The field is empty right now.** BabyChakra died in July 2025, Momspresso
   is gone, Mylo is chasing SKUs. There is no trusted, vernacular, non-selling
   pregnancy companion in India at this moment. That window will not stay open.

### Takeaway for ParentVeda

Subscription here is possible but small — assume **1–3% conversion** at
₹199–399/month if you push hard, and that is optimistic against Indian
benchmarks. Do not build the business on it.

Build it on three lines, in this order:
- **Brand-funded programmes** (Peanut's model, not BabyCenter's) — an advertiser
  funds a real feature, the mother pays nothing.
- **Third-trimester commerce** — hospital bag, nursery, postpartum. This is a
  concentrated, high-intent, high-basket window and you already have the
  Hospital Bag / Ready-for-Birth surface built.
- **Service and hospital referral** — the ₹1–3 lakh decision.

## §3. Early parenting (0–3/4 years)

### Global leaders

| Company | Scale | Model |
|---|---|---|
| **Lovevery** | **$226M revenue** on subscription Montessori play kits, $80–120 per box, ages 0–4 | **Physical subscription.** The single biggest pure-parenting consumer business in the world |
| **Nanit** | **$100M+ revenue, 1M DAU, 500k weekly actives.** Raised $50M in 2025 | Hardware ($200+) + **$10/mo Insights** subscription |
| **Owlet** | **$78.1M FY24, +45%**; Q2'25 $26.1M, +26% | Hardware. Notably: **no required subscription** — and it's growing faster than the ones that charge |
| **Huckleberry** | **5M+ families.** Sleep prediction ("SweetSpot"), AI chat guidance | Freemium → Plus/Premium subscription |
| **Napper** | $59.99/yr. Raised only $150k | Design-led sleep subscription |
| **Solid Starts** | 4M+ parents, 1M+ downloads; **~$500k/month**; IAP $19.99–99.99 | One narrow problem: starting solids |
| **Kinedu / BabySparks** | Modest | Activity-plan subscriptions |
| **Tinyhood** | ~$2M | Expert course library |

### India

| Company | Position |
|---|---|
| **FirstCry** | Dominant. See §2 |
| **Skillmatics** | **₹103 Cr FY25, +39%** — but slipped to a **₹2.5 Cr net loss**. 89% of sales are products for under-10s; **India sales up 87% to ₹58 Cr**. Physical educational products |
| **Flintobox** | STEAM activity-box subscription, the Indian Lovevery analogue. Small; skews older (2–3+) |
| **Kutuki** | ~10 lakh parents, preschool learning app. Small |
| **Sleep / feeding software** | **Nothing. No Indian Huckleberry exists.** |

### Insights

1. **0–3 is the only stage where parents reliably pay** — worldwide. Three
   reasons: the pain is **acute** (sleep deprivation is physically
   debilitating), the problem is **specific and solvable**, and feedback is
   **immediate** (the baby slept, or didn't).
2. **But look at what the winners actually sell.** Lovevery's $226M is
   *cardboard and wood*. Nanit's $100M is *cameras*. Owlet's $78M is a *sock*.
   The huge numbers in this stage are **physical**, not informational.
3. **Software-only winners exist, and they are all narrow.** Huckleberry =
   sleep. Solid Starts = weaning. Napper = naps. **None of them is a "parenting
   companion."** The generalist ones (Kinedu, BabySparks) are the small ones.
   *Breadth is what kills willingness-to-pay in this stage.*
4. **Owlet growing 26–45% with no required subscription** is a warning against
   assuming subscription is always the better model. They sell the object.
5. **India confirms the physical bias.** Skillmatics grew India sales 87% — and
   still lost money, because physical products in India carry thin margins and
   heavy CAC. Flintobox proves boxes work but not at Lovevery scale.

### Takeaway for ParentVeda

**This is your best subscription window — and the only one I would put a
subscription on with confidence.** But it must be sold as a *named acute
problem*, not as a stage.

"ParentVeda for 0–3" will not convert. **"Get your baby sleeping"** will.
Price a sleep programme at **₹1,499–2,499 (~$17–28) one-time** or **₹299/month
(~$3.40)**, and expect materially better conversion than anything in pregnancy.

You already have Feeding, Sleep, Growth and Milestone trackers built in the
parenting Tools hub. Those are the *free* surface. The paid thing is the
**programme with a plan and a human check-in** on top of them.

## §4. Older kids (≈5–12), products that talk to the child

This is a different business from the first three, and the difference is
structural: **the user is the child, the payer is the parent, and retention is
driven by the child's demand, not the parent's discipline.**

### Global leaders

| Company | Scale | Model |
|---|---|---|
| **Roblox** | **$4.9bn revenue 2025, +36%; 126.5M average DAU, peak 152M** | UGC platform + virtual currency |
| **Duolingo** | **$748M 2024, +41%; 81% subscription; guiding $1.2bn for 2026** | Consumer subscription + ads |
| **Greenlight** | **~$540M ARR 2025; ~6M family accounts.** Tiers $5.99 / $10.98 / $15.98 / $24.98 per month | **Parent-pays subscription.** Kids' debit card + money skills |
| **Lingokids** | 185M families; **$120M raise Sept 2025**; $14.99/mo or $59.99/yr; **85% of revenue from the Plus subscription** | Freemium subscription, ages 2–8 |
| **Toca Boca** | Top-grossing US kids gaming app; >$1.8M/month US IAP alone | Premium apps + IAP |
| **ABCmouse** (Age of Learning) | Large, mature | Subscription |
| **Khan Academy Kids** | Large | **Free** — philanthropically funded. Sets the price floor for everyone else |
| **Epic!** | ~$15M (2023) | Reading subscription. **Bought by Byju's for ~$500M — a catastrophic overpay** |
| **Bark** | 6M+ families; ~$5.3M US revenue Jan'24–Jul'25 | Parent-pays safety monitoring |
| **GoHenry** | ~$5/mo per child, ~6-year retention, LTV:CAC ~3:1 | Acquired by Acorns, 2023 |

### India

| Company | Outcome |
|---|---|
| **Byju's** | **$22bn valuation → insolvency, valued at zero (Oct 2024).** The largest edtech failure in history |
| **Physics Wallah** | **IPO'd Nov 2025 at ~$5.2bn. FY25 revenue ₹2,890 Cr (~$328M), +49%. Still loss-making (₹240 Cr).** But it is **exam prep for 14–18**, not a 5–12 product, and it wins on *price* (₹4,000–10,000 courses vs Byju's ₹40,000+) |
| **SplashLearn** | ₹54.9 Cr FY25. Indian-built, **US revenue** — same pattern as Inito |
| **FamPay** | **Failed structurally.** Interchange-based kids' fintech cannot work in India: UPI is free, so there is no reason for a teen to use a prepaid card, and teen spend volumes never covered the model |
| **Kutuki, Chimple** | Small |

### The regulatory fact that changes everything in India

**DPDP Rules 2025.** For anyone under 18:

- **Verifiable parental consent is mandatory** before processing any data —
  Rule 10 names DigiLocker integration as an approved method of proving the
  parent's identity *and* the parent–child relationship.
- **Tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted advertising at children are
  prohibited outright — regardless of parental consent.**
- **Penalties up to ₹200 Cr (~$23M).**
- Compliance required **before May 2027.**

Read that again: **from May 2027, an ad-funded product for Indian children is
illegal.** Category 4 in India must be subscription or commerce, full stop —
and it must ship consent infrastructure that most incumbents have not built.

### Insights

1. **Two archetypes win, and only two.** (a) **Entertainment-grade engagement**
   — Roblox, Toca Boca, Duolingo. You are competing with games for the child's
   attention and you must win on craft. (b) **Parent-anxiety subscription** —
   Greenlight, Bark, GoHenry. The parent buys control, safety, or a life skill;
   the child supplies the pull that stops the parent churning. **Greenlight's
   $540M ARR is the most attractive model in this entire report.**
2. **The middle — "educational content for kids" — is where money dies.** Epic
   at $15M. SplashLearn at ₹55 Cr. Byju's at zero. Khan Academy Kids is free
   and excellent, which permanently caps what anyone can charge for the
   generic version.
3. **Indian edtech for under-12s is a graveyard,** and the survivor (PW) won by
   going *older* and *10× cheaper*, not by going younger.
4. **FamPay's failure is the single most transferable Indian lesson in this
   report.** A model that works abroad can be structurally impossible here
   because of local rails. Greenlight monetises interchange; India made
   payments free. **Check the rails before importing a model.**

### Takeaway for ParentVeda

**Do not build this.** It is a different company: different user, different
craft discipline (you would be hiring game designers), different compliance
stack, different CAC.

The *one* thing that argues the other way is that CAC is the whole problem in
kids' products, and you would already own the parent relationship from birth.
That is a real asset — but it is a **2028+ option you preserve**, not a 2026
build. Preserve it by holding the family graph (child DOB, name, interests)
cleanly and building DPDP-grade consent early, since you will need it for the
parenting stage anyway.

## §5. Cross-cutting: where subscription works and where it doesn't

The pattern across all four stages:

**Consumers pay for a specific outcome inside a short window of acute pain.
They do not pay for a companion, a stage, or access to content.**

| Stage | Pain intensity | Window | Substitute is free? | Verdict on subscription |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Preconception | High but **private** | 3–12 mo | Doctor / Google / family | **No.** Monetise via clinic + diagnostics referral |
| Pregnancy | Medium, anxious | 9 mo, hard stop | BabyCenter, YouTube, WhatsApp aunties | **Weak.** Brand-funded + commerce + services |
| Early parenting 0–3 | **Very high, acute, nightly** | Recurring for 3 yrs | Nothing good in Hindi | **Yes — this is the one.** Sell named problems |
| Kids 5–12 | Parent anxiety, not pain | Years | Khan Academy free, YouTube | **Only as parent-anxiety utility** (safety/money), not content |

### The headwind nobody in this category talks about

**India's total fertility rate is now ~1.9 — below the 2.1 replacement level.**
The birth rate fell to 16.55 in 2025, down 1.19% year on year, and it is
falling fastest in exactly the urban, smartphone-owning, English-or-Hindi
literate segment this product serves.

Two consequences, and they point in opposite directions:

- **The top of your funnel shrinks every year.** Any plan whose growth
  assumption is "more babies" is wrong. Growth has to come from share and from
  revenue per family, not from cohort size.
- **But revenue per child rises.** Fewer children per family means more spend
  per child — which is precisely why FirstCry, Cloudnine and Skillmatics are
  growing 18–39% into a shrinking birth cohort. Declining fertility is *bad*
  for ad-funded reach models and *good* for premium products.

That asymmetry is itself an argument for the §7 table: reach-monetisation gets
harder from here, outcome-monetisation gets easier.

### Indian pricing reality

- Global norm is **$9.99/mo or $59.99/yr**. In India that lands at ₹880/mo,
  which almost nobody will pay for parenting information.
- Realistic Indian ceilings: **₹199–399/month (~$2.25–4.50)** for a recurring
  subscription; **₹1,499–2,999 (~$17–34)** for a one-time outcome programme.
- **One-time beats recurring in India** for anything problem-shaped. Indians
  buy a *course* or a *fix*; they resist a meter running. Note that the
  parenting-app market globally already sees **61.5% of subscription revenue
  from annual plans**, and India skews harder that way still.
- Freemium conversion benchmarks: **3–5% typical globally, 6–8% for top
  performers, and emerging markets run below 0.2% median.** Plan for **1–2%**
  in India and be delighted by 3%.
- **Hard paywalls convert at ~12.1% D35 vs 2.2% for freemium** — but a hard
  paywall is incompatible with "a feature is never hidden," which is a stated
  invariant of this product. That is a real trade-off, not a free lunch: you
  are choosing trust and reach over conversion rate, and you should choose it
  knowingly and monetise elsewhere.

## §6. The four gaps worth taking

**Gap 1 — India has no pre-clinic fertility layer.**
Inito sells a ₹8,000–9,000 monitor into the US. Indian women get stigma, then
a clinic that starts at ₹1.5 lakh. Nothing sits in between answering *"is this
normal, and should I see someone yet?"* in Hindi, privately.
→ Build guidance + preparation + routing. Revenue is clinic and diagnostics
referral, not subscription.

**Gap 2 — the Indian pregnancy trust layer is vacant right now.**
BabyChakra died July 2025. Momspresso is gone. Mylo is selling shampoo. Nobody
owns vernacular, non-selling, clinically-careful pregnancy guidance in India
today. This is the most defensible position available and it is temporarily
unoccupied.
→ Take it with content and community. Monetise with brand-funded programmes,
third-trimester commerce, and hospital/service referral. **Not** subscription.

**Gap 3 — there is no Indian Huckleberry, and this is the best money.**
Baby sleep is the highest-pain, highest-willingness-to-pay problem in consumer
parenting globally, and India has no product for it in any language.
→ **Build a named sleep programme.** ₹1,499–2,499 one-time or ₹299/month. This
is where I would put subscription, and nowhere else.

**Gap 4 — DPDP creates a compliance moat for kids' products from May 2027.**
Verifiable parental consent, DigiLocker-based, plus an outright ban on
behavioural ads to minors. Most incumbents have not built this.
→ Not a 2026 build. **Preserve the option** by holding the family graph cleanly
and shipping consent infrastructure with the parenting stage.

## §7. What to offer, and what not to

| Category | Offer | Do **not** offer | Charge? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Preconception** | Private guidance, cycle understanding, clinic-prep, vetted clinic & diagnostics routing, partner content | Standalone TTC subscription; personalised probability of conception; anything social or public | **No consumer charge.** Referral + diagnostics commission |
| **2. Pregnancy** | Vernacular trust layer, weekly companion, community, Ask Veda, hospital-bag → commerce, service booking | Ad-funded model copied from BabyCenter; a broad "premium tier"; own-label D2C SKUs (that's Mylo's grave, and FirstCry owns the shelf) | **Mostly no.** Brand-funded + commerce + service margin. A thin ₹199/mo tier is optional, not load-bearing |
| **3. Early parenting 0–3** | **Named problem programmes: sleep first, then feeding/solids.** Free trackers underneath. Physical attach later (kits, monitors) | A generic "0–3 companion subscription"; competing with FirstCry on nappies | **Yes — the one stage to charge.** ₹1,499–2,499 one-time per programme, or ₹299/mo |
| **4. Kids 5–12** | Nothing yet. Preserve the family graph + build DPDP-grade consent | A kids' learning app; a kids' card/fintech (FamPay proved the rails don't support it); anything ad-funded at children | **N/A in 2026.** If ever: parent-anxiety utility, ₹199–499/mo |

---

## §8. The three sentences that matter

1. **In India, information does not sell; outcomes and objects do** — which is
   why FirstCry (₹7,660 Cr) and Cloudnine (₹2,000 Cr) are the winners and every
   content app in the category is dead or tiny.
2. **The only stage with genuine consumer willingness-to-pay is 0–3, and only
   for a named acute problem** — sell sleep, not parenting.
3. **Before importing any foreign model, check the local rails** — FamPay died
   because UPI is free, and an ad-funded kids product will die because DPDP
   bans it from May 2027.
