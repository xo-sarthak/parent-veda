# BAND A (0 to 12 months): Crying and Temperament
Not "behaviour" yet. This is what a 0-1 parent sees. Reassurance-first, with a mandatory safety layer.

### A1. Is my baby's crying normal? [ARTICLE]
- Why: crying rises from ~2 weeks, peaks at 6-8 weeks, eases after. It is normal development, not a fault in you or the baby.
- In the moment [STEP-LIST]: check the basics (hungry, wind, nappy, too hot/cold, tired, overstimulated), then hold/rock/white noise, then if nothing works and baby is fed and safe, it is okay to pause.
- What NOT to do [CARDS]: don't assume it's always hunger and overfeed; don't blame yourself; don't compare to a "good baby."
- When to worry [FLAGGED CALLOUT]: high-pitched or weak cry, fever, poor feeding, fewer wet nappies, floppy or hard to wake -> doctor.
- Age note: 0-4 months. Related: A5 coping, Sleep section.
- Video placeholder: the normal crying curve, expert.

### A2. Why won't my baby settle? [ARTICLE]
- Why: babies settle differently; often tired-but-fighting-sleep, overstimulated, or needing contact.
- In the moment [STEP-LIST]: dim and quieten, swaddle/hold, rhythmic motion, white noise, offer a feed if due.
- What NOT to do [CARDS]: don't keep switching methods every 30 seconds; don't add more stimulation.
- Links: Sleep (settling), Feeding (comfort feeding).
- Age note: 0-6 months.

### A3. You can't spoil a newborn [SHORT ARTICLE]
- Why: responding to a young baby's cries builds security, it does not create a habit of "spoiling."
- Counters the joint-family line "you're carrying him too much / picking him up too much."
- Age note: 0-6 months. India note: name the family-pressure reality directly and kindly.

### A4. Understanding your baby's temperament [CARDS]
- What: easy / spirited / slow-to-warm as gentle descriptions, not labels or verdicts.
- Each card: what it looks like, what this baby needs more of, what helps.
- Age note: 0-12 months. Guardrail: never a diagnosis, never "difficult baby."

### A5. Coping when the crying is too much [ARTICLE + FLAGGED CALLOUT] — MANDATORY SAFETY PAGE
- Why: relentless crying is exhausting and can push any parent to the edge; this is common and human.
- What to do [STEP-LIST]: if you feel overwhelmed, put the baby down somewhere safe (on their back in the cot), step away, breathe, come back in a few minutes; call someone.
- The hard line [FLAGGED CALLOUT]: never shake a baby; shouting or rough handling makes it worse and can cause serious harm. Where to get help now (helpline config value, REQUIRED-TO-CONFIRM).
- Age note: 0-12 months. This page must be reachable, calm, and never carry a product or upsell.

### A6. When crying needs a doctor [FLAGGED CALLOUT]
- Honest red flags: crying that persists strongly past 4 months especially with feeding and sleeping trouble; signs of illness; sudden change in cry.
- Routes to pediatric consult.

Band A tools/links: reuse `pp_what_changed` for "won't settle / crying more" entries; link Sleep and Feeding. No script library here (pre-verbal). Consult: pediatric, on A5/A6.

# BAND B (1 to 3 years): the core — independence, tantrums, specific behaviours

## Door B1: The stubborn / ziddi child [the dominant India door]

### B1.1 How to handle a ziddi bachcha [ARTICLE]
- Why: at 1-3 the child is realizing they are separate and testing independence; "ziddi" is that drive, not defiance to be broken.
- In the moment [STEP-LIST]: stay calm, offer two acceptable choices, acknowledge the want ("you really want the phone"), hold the limit kindly, redirect.
- What NOT to do [CARDS]: don't turn it into a power struggle, don't cave after saying no, don't shame ("bad boy"), don't hit.
- Words to use [SCRIPT box -> library]: "You can have the red cup or the blue cup." "I know you want it. The answer is still no, and I'm here."
- When to worry [FLAGGED CALLOUT]: aggression that injures, no language by the expected range, extreme rigidity -> consult.
- Age note: 1.5-3y. Related: B2 tantrums, connection activities.
- Video placeholder: "ziddi = independence" reframe, expert.

### B1.2 Stubborn vs strong-willed [SHORT ARTICLE]
- Reframe for parents: the same trait that is hard now (persistence, knowing their mind) is a strength later. How to guide it, not crush it.

### B1.3 Choices within limits [STEP-LIST]
- The practical technique: give two options you're happy with, so the child gets agency and you keep the boundary. Worked examples for dressing, eating, leaving the park.

### B1.4 Stubborn and aggressive [ARTICLE]
- Why + underlying causes (frustration, tiredness, no words yet), clear boundaries, replacement behaviours, and an honest when-to-consult line.

### B1.5 Am I too strict or too soft? [ARTICLE]
- The warm/firm balance, consistency matters more than strictness, what over-strict and over-soft each cost. India note: navigating differing family views.

## Door B2: Tantrums and big emotions [the daily-life door]

### B2.1 What a tantrum actually is, and why 1-3 [ARTICLE]
- Why: tantrums are how a child shows upset/frustration when tired, hungry, or thwarted, and when language can't keep up with feeling; they peak 1-3 and fade with age.
- In the moment [STEP-LIST]: keep yourself calm, keep the child safe, don't reason mid-storm, be present, let it pass, reconnect after.
- What NOT to do [CARDS]: don't shout back (they copy it), don't give in to end it (teaches it works), don't punish the feeling.
- Words to use [SCRIPT box -> library]: "You're so upset. I'm right here. We'll wait together."
- When to worry [FLAGGED CALLOUT]: tantrums worsening after 4, self-injury, breath-holding that alarms you -> consult.
- Age note: 1-4y. Related: co-regulation activities, calm-down corner.
- Video placeholder: in-the-moment response demo.

### B2.2 Tantrum vs crying vs meltdown [SHORT ARTICLE]
- The difference (a meltdown is overwhelm, not manipulation), so parents respond to the right thing.

### B2.3 What to do in the moment [STEP-LIST]
- The clean 5-step: safe, calm, present, name it, reconnect. Printable/saveable.

### B2.4 Public-place tantrums [ARTICLE]
- The supermarket/mandir/family-function scenario; how to handle it without the audience-pressure; India note: the knowing-aunty reality, you're not being judged as much as you fear.

### B2.5 Co-regulation and naming feelings [STEP-LIST / links to activities]

## Door B3: Specific behaviours [the "he's doing X" lookup door]
Each is a full ARTICLE on the fixed skeleton (why / in-the-moment steps / what-not-to-do / words-to-use / when-to-worry / age + related activity):

- **Hitting and biting** — more motor control than words; stop the hand gently, "I won't let you hit," name the feeling, redirect; don't hit back or force a fake sorry; worry if daily/injuring/past 3-4.
- **Throwing** — testing cause-and-effect; give what CAN be thrown (soft ball), redirect; don't punish exploration.
- **Screaming** — for effect or overwhelm; lower your own voice, name it; don't out-shout.
- **Anger and aggression** — co-regulate first, name the emotion, calm-down strategies; model calm; worry if it escalates or injures.
- **Sharing and grabbing** — sharing is developmentally hard before ~3; turn-taking games, don't force sharing; set timers.
- **Sibling fights** — stay neutral, coach don't referee, one-on-one time; don't always blame the elder.
- **Not listening / defiance** — get down to eye level, one clear instruction, follow through; don't repeat-and-threaten.
- **Clinginess and separation anxiety** — peaks 15-18 months, a sign of secure attachment; short goodbyes, a ritual, never sneak out; don't shame.
- **Whining** — often tired/connection-seeking; respond to the need not the tone, teach the "big voice"; don't cave to whining.
- **Lying (older end)** — often wishful/testing, not moral failure at this age; stay calm, make truth safe; don't trap or label "liar."

## Door B4: Screen time [own door, spans B into C]

### B4.1 It's the sudden removal, not the screen [ARTICLE]
- Why: the meltdown is the ambush of an abrupt cut-off, not the screen itself.
- In the moment [STEP-LIST]: give a warning ("two more minutes"), a visual timer, a next-thing to move to, end on a natural stopping point.
- What NOT to do [CARDS]: don't snatch it away with no warning; don't use screens as the only calming tool.
- Words to use [SCRIPT box]: "When the timer beeps, screen goes to sleep. Then we'll have our snack."
- Age note: 1.5y+. Video placeholder: transition-without-tears demo.

### B4.2 Healthy limits by age [CARDS / CHART]
- Age-banded rough guidance (India-real, non-shaming), what quality vs passive screen means.

### B4.3 Transitions off screens without a meltdown [STEP-LIST]
- The warning + timer + landing-activity method, in detail.

### B4.4 Screens and Indian family reality [ARTICLE]
- Grandparents handing the phone, screens at mealtimes, everyone on devices; how to set a family norm kindly.

## Door B5: Discipline without hitting [the honest, India-relevant door]

### B5.1 Guiding behaviour without spanking, and why [ARTICLE]
- Why plainly: at this age children don't connect a smack to the behaviour; hitting models that hitting is how you handle anger; it's no more effective than calmer methods. Said kindly, not preachily, aware this is a cultural norm in India.
- What to do instead [STEP-LIST]: connect, set the limit, natural/logical consequence, consistency.
- Age note: 1-6y.
- Video placeholder: calm-discipline demo.

### B5.2 Redirection, consistency, natural consequences [STEP-LIST]
- The three everyday tools with worked examples.

### B5.3 Time-outs done right [SHORT ARTICLE]
- If used: brief (~1 min per year), a calm-down not a banishment, and why it only fits ~3+ (younger kids don't grasp it).

### B5.4 Handling grandparents and differing discipline styles [ARTICLE]
- Very India: aligning with elders without conflict, presenting a united front, picking your battles.

# Activities: Connection and regulation [NEW, via activity engine]
Each a named ACTIVITY page (what it is / steps / when to use / age):
- **Calm-down corner** — a chosen soft spot with 2-3 comfort items, a reset not a punishment. Steps: pick it together, name it, add items, practice when calm. Age 2+.
- **Balloon breathing** — hands on belly, breathe in like inflating a balloon, slow out. For mid-frustration or wind-down. Age 2.5+. Video placeholder.
- **Name the feeling** — you narrate what you see ("you're angry the tower fell"), plus an emotion-faces mini game. Age 18m+.
- **Glitter / calm jar** — shake, watch it settle, breathe while it settles. Make-it steps + use-it steps. Age 2.5+.
- **Connection games (fill the tank)** — 5-10 min child-led play / rough-and-tumble / peekaboo, meeting the connection need so acting-out drops. Age: all bands (age-scaled).
- **Feelings check-in** — a simple daily "how do you feel" with faces or cards. Age 3+.
Each tagged by band so Band A sees only age-right ones (mostly connection games), Band B/C see the full set.

# Guardrails (the ones this section needs most)
- Reframe, don't pathologize: stubbornness = independence, tantrums = normal development.
- Honest on discipline: no spanking, and say why, without lecturing.
- Don't overstate the framing as clinical research: positive-discipline is a philosophy, present it honestly.
- Real red-flag route to a psychologist for the minority (persistent extreme aggression, head-banging, regression, self-injury), without pathologizing the everyday.
- Band A safety layer (never shake, cope-with-crying) is mandatory and upsell-free.
- India-real throughout: ziddi, joint family, grandparents, physical-punishment norms; not a Western gentle-parenting import.
