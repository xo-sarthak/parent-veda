# How ParentVeda talks in Parenting — the voice guide

Written 2026-10-07 for the parenting pass. Source of truth for WHAT to change is the parenting gap analysis
(`Parenting-Gap-Analysis-WhatToExpect-theAsianparent-vs-ParentVeda.pdf`, version 1, 2 October 2026, and its
`.xlsx`). This file is HOW every word in the parenting stage is written while that work is done: door pages, door
tiles and heroes, the age page, daily tips, tools, buttons, empty screens, messages.

It is the trying-to-conceive voice (`docs/TTC-VOICE.md`, approved 2026-09-26) and the pregnancy voice
(`docs/PREG-VOICE.md`, 2026-09-29) carried across, with the reader changed and the parenting rules from the gap
analysis added. Where the guides agree, this one does not repeat the reasoning: read §2, §4, §5 and §8 of the
pregnancy guide; they hold here word for word.

The user's brief, in their words: *warm, comforting, hugging, safe, reassuring; she understands it in one go and is
never confused; it must not feel written by AI.*

---

## 1. Who we are talking to

A parent in India with a child from birth to six. Usually the mother, often at 3 am with one hand free; sometimes
the father, a grandparent or the didi who looks after the baby, reading over her shoulder. She is tired more often
than not. Her mother-in-law, the paediatrician, the WhatsApp group and the internet all say different things, and
she is quietly afraid she is getting it wrong.

We sound like **an older sister who has raised two children and happens to be a good paediatrician's friend.** On
her side, calm, specific, never shocked. We tell her what is normal first, what to do next, and when to call the
doctor. We never sound like a textbook, a lecture, a scoreboard or a shop.

## 2. The rules

All fifteen rules of the pregnancy guide (§2) apply: "you"; contractions; short sentences; plain words first and
the medical word in brackets; the comforting true thing early; name the feeling once, then help; facts, not
cheerleading; never her fault; no dashes as punctuation; no capitals, exclamation marks or emoji; no clever lines;
doctor lines calm and exact; short screen text; Indian where it helps; about the same length.

What changes for parenting:

1. **Two people are in every sentence: her and the child.** "You" is the parent. The child is never "the baby" in
   the abstract when we know who it is.
2. **How we name the child** (decided by the user, 2026-10-07):
   - On pages built for HER child (the age page, the home, tools, messages, the checklist), use the child's name
     where it reads naturally, and he or she from the profile: `ChildProfileStore.they / their / them / theyCap /
     theirCap`. When she did not give the sex, those getters return the name or "your baby" by themselves; never
     write "he/she" or "they" by hand.
   - On door pages (shared by every family), write neutrally: "your baby" under one, "your child" or "your toddler"
     after. Never "him" or "her" on a door page. A door name is neutral too: "Keeping your child safe", not
     "Keeping him safe".
   - **How to stay neutral without sounding stiff** (added 2026-10-07 for the door voice pass): say "your baby"
     once, then let the sentence carry it; turn a pronoun into the thing itself ("a feed" not "his feed", "the
     nappy" not "his nappy"); talk to her ("hold your baby close", "you'll notice"); use the plural when the line is
     about babies in general ("Most babies this age wake twice a night"); join two short sentences so the noun is
     said once. Do not write singular "they" for the child, and do not repeat "your baby" three times in a
     paragraph: rewrite the sentence instead.
   - "Her" meaning the mother on her own pages stays "you".
3. **Normal first, then the range.** "Most babies this age wake two or three times a night" before any number. Ages
   are ranges ("between 9 and 12 months"), never a deadline.
4. **No scoreboard.** No "behind", "ahead", "should be", "late", "advanced". Milestones are "usually by now",
   "starting now" and "coming soon". The only time we name a missing skill is the red-flag line, and it routes to
   the doctor calmly ("It's worth asking your paediatrician at the next visit. Early help works best.").
5. **Never compare to other children,** her friends' or the WhatsApp group's.
6. **Family advice is corrected kindly,** because the person who said it loves the child. "Your mother-in-law may
   suggest a little honey for the cough. It isn't safe before one, even a drop; here is what helps instead."
7. **Feeding without judgement.** Breast, bottle, formula and mixed are all feeding. Formula is described honestly
   and never promoted (India's IMS Act): no brand praise, no "best formula", no bottle as a default.
8. **Never a dose.** Medicines say what they are for and how to give them safely; the amount is always "your doctor
   sets the dose by your child's weight".
9. **Fever and the newborn.** Any fever under three months is a same-day doctor visit; that line is never softened.
10. **Her own body counts.** The mother's recovery, mood and sleep are written to with the same warmth as the
    child's. Low mood after birth is common and treatable; the crisis path (112, Tele-MANAS 14416) is one tap away
    wherever a page touches frightening thoughts.
11. **The father is a parent, not a helper.** Pages for him say "you" to him and give him a real job ("take one night
    feed this week"), never "help her with".

## 3. What the gap analysis adds for parenting

- **A short answer at the top of every page** that answers the title in two or three sentences.
- **Headings as her questions** where a page explains something ("Why does this happen?", "What helps?", "When
  should I call the doctor?").
- **"Call the doctor if" on every age page**, three to five lines for that age, red flags only.
- **"Where this comes from"** under every age page: IAP (growth, vaccines), WHO (growth standards, breastfeeding),
  AAP and CDC (milestones), the Ministry of Health's home-based newborn care. Named bodies only; no invented study.
- **No claim we can't back.** Bylines stay "ParentVeda editorial" until a real, contracted clinician has read the
  page. No "reviewed by", "doctor approved", "clinically proven" or "verified" unless it happened. Anything that is
  not made yet says "Opening soon", never a price, a rating or a count.
- **India first.** Rupees, chemist, creche, didi, dadi and nani, joint families, monsoon and heat, Indian foods by
  name (khichdi, ragi, dal ka paani), government schemes and the UIP schedule beside IAP. US or Singapore facts are
  rewritten for India, never translated.

## 4. The banned list

Everything in the pregnancy guide's §4, plus: "should be able to", "behind", "ahead of", "advanced for", "late
talker" (as our label), "good baby", "bad habit" (as our label), "spoil", "sleep training" as a verdict, "mommy
brain", "supermom", "mama", "little one" more than once a page, "precious", "bundle of joy".

## 5. What must never change

The pregnancy guide's §5, word for word: facts, safety lines and their urgency, ids and keys, the Hindi side of
any shipped pair (new work is English), and no copying. For parenting the "no copying" rule names What to Expect
and theAsianparent: they show us what to cover; no run of eight words may match theirs.

>

>
> After: How Meera is doing. Nothing is expected of her yet, and that's how it should be.

**4. A warning** (fever)
> Before: If he is under 3 months and has a temperature of 38°C or more, see a doctor.
>
> After: If your baby is under three months and has a temperature of 38°C or more, see a doctor today, even if your
> baby seems well.

## 7. How each rewrite is checked

The pregnancy guide's §7, plus two checks for parenting:

1. **No hard-coded pronoun on a door page.** A script greps door content for " him", " his ", " he " and " her "
   outside quotes and lists every hit; each one is rewritten or justified.
2. **No scoreboard words** from §4 in the changed strings.

## 8. Technical notes

The pregnancy guide's §8 holds. One more: on screens built for her child, build the sentence from the getters
(`'${c.theyCap} may start to roll this month.'`), never by `isBoy ? 'he' : 'she'` inline, so "not given" is
handled once.
