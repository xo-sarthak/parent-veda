## 1. New page: "Going back to work"
Area: `childcare` (tab "Care while you work"). Bands: 0–12 months. Format: STEP-LIST.

**Intro.** Going back to work with a small baby is hard on both of you, and it gets easier. A little planning in the weeks before makes the first days calmer.
1. **Know your rights.** REQUIRED_REVIEW (legal).
   - Under the Maternity Benefit Act, most women working in establishments with 10 or more employees get **26 weeks of paid maternity leave** for the first two children.
   - Workplaces with **50 or more employees** must provide a creche, or access to one, and **four creche visits a day**, counting the nursing breaks.
   - Many employers allow work from home after the leave. Ask.
   - Link: Papers.
2. **Start the bottle or cup two to three weeks before.**
   - Someone else offers it, once a day, when he's calm and a little hungry, not starving.
   - Expressed milk first.
   - **Many babies refuse the bottle from their mother, and take it from someone else.**
   - From 6 months, an open cup or a spoon works too.
   - Link: Feeding's bottle page.
3. **Pumping at work:**
   - pump as often as he'd feed, usually every three hours
   - store the milk in a clean, closed container, in a fridge, or in a cool bag with ice packs for the trip home
   - Link: Feeding's pumping and storage pages. REQUIRED_REVIEW (storage times).
4. **The first week with the carer:**
   - a few short practice days before you start
   - his routine written down: feeds, naps, what calms him
   - your number and the doctor's number
   - Link: "Settling in with a new carer".
5. **Feed when you're together:** early morning, evening and night feeds keep your supply going.
6. **The guilt:** most working mothers feel it. Children of working parents do well when the care is warm and steady. How you are with him counts more than the hours.

**Doctor callout:** If he's losing weight, feeding much less, or you're struggling with low mood, talk to your doctor.

## 2. New page: "You two, after the baby"
Area: `grandparents` (tab "Grandparents"), next to `fam_couple_on_same_page`. Bands: every age. Format: CARDS.

**Intro.** A baby changes a couple more than almost anything. Less sleep, less time, and two tired people. Arguing more in the first year is very common, and so is finding your way back.
- **The fights:** most are about sleep, chores and the in-laws. Agree on one thing at a time, at a calm moment, not at 3 am.
- **Divide it out loud:** list the jobs (night feeds, nappies, cooking, appointments) and share them. Unspoken expectations cause the most hurt.
- **Ten minutes a day:** tea together after he sleeps, with no phones and no baby talk for a few minutes.
- **Sex and closeness, when you're ready:**
  - there's no deadline
  - low desire is very common while breastfeeding, from hormones and tiredness
  - pain or dryness is worth telling your doctor about
  - REQUIRED_REVIEW
- **Contraception:** you can get pregnant before your period returns. Link: You, Maa's contraception page.
- **When it's more than tiredness:** if there's shouting, fear, control, or anyone is hurt, that isn't a normal rough patch. The safety callout on "When you and your husband disagree" applies. Women's helpline 181; police 112.

## 3. New page: "Doing it on your own"
Area: `fathers` (tab "For fathers"; the tab blurb gains "and parents on their own"). Bands: every age. Format: STEP-LIST.

**Intro.** Whether you're a single mother or father, through choice, separation or loss, you're doing two people's work. This page is for you.
1. **Ask for help, and name it.** "Can you take him from 5 to 7 on Saturday" works better than "let me know if I can help".
2. **Build a small circle:**
   - one or two people who can come at short notice
   - a neighbour
   - a parent group
3. **The papers and the money:**
   - a single parent can apply for his birth certificate, Aadhaar and passport on their own
   - guardianship, maintenance and scheme papers are in the Papers door
   - REQUIRED_REVIEW (legal)
4. **Keep one thing that's yours:** a walk, a call, an hour on Sunday.
5. **If you've lost your partner:** grief and a baby together are a lot. It's all right to need help.

**Callout:** "If you feel low most days, can't sleep or eat, or can't cope, talk to your doctor, or call Tele-MANAS on 14416, free, any hour."
